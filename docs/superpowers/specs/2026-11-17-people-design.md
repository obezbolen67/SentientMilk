# The people in her life (0.31)

## Why

Her thoughts called the user "she" (2026-11-17, a rumination: "She said NO to more sleep and now she's got
assignments"), while other thoughts called him by his name. Nothing in her mind's prompts ever said who the user
is: they said "the user", with no name and no pronoun, among a persona full of "she". Chats knew his name only
from lasting memory ("His name is <name> (the app shows him as "plays")"), which 0.30 took out of every prompt:
measured on a copy of her brain, the name came back for "what's my name?" and never for "hey".

The user asked for how a human remembers people, not a settings field: "an approach so she can remember
people, understand about who conversation is and refer to them (including me)".

## How people remember people, and what she does

| A person | Her |
|---|---|
| Recognizes a face: the whole person comes to mind, by being there, not by topic | The app's handle ("user: plays" in a turn's context) is her face: she recognizes who goes by it and has them in mind for the chat (WHO YOU'RE WITH, in the session's snapshot) and in her thoughts about it |
| Remembers moments with who was in them | An exchange is kept as "<name> said: ..."; older "The user said" ones read with the name of whom the app's user is |
| Knows people as a gist distilled from moments, settled in sleep | Each night she goes over what happened and updates what she knows about each person in it (name, other names, pronoun, a gist in her own words, the handles they go by); one light call, thinking on |
| Hears a name and thinks of that person; a new name is a stranger | People named in a message come with it in her note; unknown names stay unknown until sleep makes sense of them; apps named after people are not people |
| Closeness decides what lasts | Someone met on few days and not seen for 90 days is forgotten; whoever is around stays |
| Remembers who they met before | Once, at first start, she meets the people of her past: her old facts and memories go through the same step |

## Not

- No settings field, no table of rules about the user, no tool to save people: she learns them.
- No biography in a prompt: the gist is what she keeps in mind when someone is there (her story, a
  biography, leaked into 7 of 12 replies; 0.30 measured and removed it).

## Also fixed (found in the same audit)

- What she does in tasks is remembered again (her mind's "event" moments came from the journal, gone in
  0.30): a task's end is a moment of hers.
- A rumination no longer brings back the moment it is about, nor her own thoughts on it, only what
  happened (it was given twice, with her last thought on it: the loops the user noticed, "you're so
  centered on this watcher thing").
- The sleep prompt no longer promises "what they know and keep".
- todo_write takes "in_progress" and "completed" (8 of her 38 calls were refused for those words).

## Measured (2026-11-17, copies of her brain, mind and long-term memory; her light and main models)

- The 13:14 rumination, 6 thoughts each: before, 6 of 6 called the user "she"; with who she talks with
  named and the rumination bringing back what happened (not itself, not her own thoughts on it), 0 of 6
  ("he" in 5). With only the name added, 5 of 6 still said "she": they copied the old thought that came
  back with it. So a rumination now brings back exchanges, events and insights only.
- Meeting her past (150 moments, her old facts): quick (thinking off) she met 1 to 16 of the same people
  across runs; thinking on, 10 to 16 every run, the user (he) with "plays" in MilkChan each time, three friends and
  a group of them. Given her persona, her story's mother and father were met as people of her
  life in 2 of 3 runs; without it, 0 of 2. "plays" given as one of his names brought him to mind wherever
  the word was: a handle is never a name now.
- A fresh chat, "what's my name?": before "Plays. That's what the plate on the door says"; now "<name>.
  Plays, to everyone who isn't your mother yelling up the stairs." "hey" stays a plain hello, with no
  biography in it.
